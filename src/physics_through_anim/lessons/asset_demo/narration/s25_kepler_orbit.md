# Scene 25 — A Kepler orbit

TODO narration (draft): A planet traces an ellipse, with the Sun not at the centre
but at one focus. The gravity arrow always points straight at the Sun, and the
radius vector — the line from Sun to planet — sweeps around as the planet moves.
Near the Sun, at periapsis, the planet races along; far away, at apoapsis, it
crawls. But watch the two shaded wedges: the area the radius vector sweeps in a
fixed slice of time is exactly the same whether the planet is close or far. That
is Kepler's second law — equal areas in equal times — and it is really angular
momentum being conserved. The graph on the right tracks the distance r as it rises
to apoapsis and falls back.
